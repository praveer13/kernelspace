# T7.L4 — Unit Economics: $/Mtok, tok/MW, and the 545% Margin Day

_Track T7: Economics & SLO Engineering · ~30 min · kernelspace_

> DeepSeek published a full day of production arithmetic: 226 nodes, $87k cost, 608B input tokens, a 545% theoretical cost profit margin (profit over cost, at list prices). Reproduce it from first principles, and you can price any serving business on a napkin.
Every previous lesson had a price tag; this one reads it. The units:

- **$/Mtok** — cost per million tokens (input and output priced separately; outputs cost ~3–5× inputs because decode is serial and cache-hit input is nearly free).
- **tok/s/$** and **tok/MW** — throughput per dollar and per megawatt. The second is the datacenter's real constraint: power, not GPUs, is what you can't buy more of this decade. NVIDIA's own pitch is a vendor claim at one stated interactivity point: Vera Rubin NVL72 plus LPX "up to 35x higher TPS per megawatt at 400 TPS per user" versus GB200 NVL72, to be decomposed, not recited. The measured counterpoint is InferenceMAX's ~8× tokens per MW for a GB200 NVL72 at FP4 over a single-node H200 at FP8 on DeepSeek-R1 at 30 tok/s/user, so the gap mixes hardware and number format.
- **Cost stack** — GPU-hours (dominant: an H100 rents on demand at a median $3.49 per GPU-hour across 41 providers on 2026-10-04 per [GetDeploying](https://getdeploying.com/reference/cloud-gpu/nvidia-h100), a dated price that moves daily; ~$0.7–1/hr amortized owned), power+cooling (~15–25% on top), CPU/network/storage (~10%), people (you don't price in yet).

Reproduce DeepSeek's day (open-infra index, Feb 2025): **226.75 nodes on average (8×H800 each)** → 1,814 GPUs avg × $2/GPU/hr × 24 h = **$87,072 cost** (the $2 is DeepSeek's stated leasing assumption). Served **608B input tokens** (56.3% KV-cache hit) and **168B output tokens**. R1 list prices: $0.14/M input hit, $0.55/M input miss, $2.19/M output → revenue ≈ $562,027 (DeepSeek's own total; the token counts are rounded). What DeepSeek calls a **cost profit margin of 545%** is profit over cost: ($562,027 − $87,072) ÷ $87,072 = 5.45, so price is about 6.45× cost. The same totals as a gross margin, 1 − cost ÷ revenue, are **84.5%**. Both are *list-price theoretical*: DeepSeek says actual revenue was substantially lower (V3 is priced below R1, web and app are free, off-peak discounts apply). The point is the shape.

## The napkin model that prices anything

One line per direction:
- **Cost per useful token ≈ (GPU-hours × $/hr) ÷ tokens delivered within SLO.** Those tokens = goodput × time — which is why [T7.L1](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t7.l1)'s objective function is also the cost function. Output from requests that miss their latency targets costs GPU-hours but does not count as a useful token. Utilization below SLO collapse is the whole game: an idle GPU bills the same as a full one.
- **Price is revenue per token, and two relations tie it to cost.** A *markup* gives price = cost × (1 + markup); a *gross margin* m gives price = cost ÷ (1 − m). DeepSeek's 545% is a markup (price ≈ 6.45× cost), which is the same day as a gross margin of 84.5%; this lesson says "margin" only with its form named. Units stay explicit: price and cost in $/Mtok, a GPU's rent C in $/GPU-hour, throughput in tok/s. To earn gross margin m at price P ($/Mtok), each GPU must deliver at least tok/s = C × 10⁶ ÷ (3,600 × P × (1 − m)) inside its SLO: at C = $2 (the round rent this lesson assumes), P = $0.60 and m = 50%, that is 2 × 10⁶ ÷ (3,600 × 0.60 × 0.5) ≈ **1,850 tok/s per GPU**. Then read where your operating point on the [T7.L3](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t7.l3) frontier sits against it.

Worked example, SGLang's DeepSeek reproduction ([T6.L2](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t6.l2)): their blog (May 2025) reports 22.3k output tok/s per node on 8×H100 nodes, measured on 9 decode nodes with the prefill side assumed unlimited. Per node-day: 22.3e3 × 86400 = 1.93G output tokens. At $2/GPU/hr × 8 GPUs × 24 = $384/node-day → **$0.20/M output tokens** (their published estimate is $0.20/1M; the blog does not state the GPU-hour price behind it). That is one decode node priced alone, at the throughput end of the frontier: the same blog lists TTFT of 2–5 seconds and ITL of about 100 ms (ten tokens a second per user) as the limit of that operating point, so $0.20 is not a cost per token inside a tight SLO. The same node-day at a later reading of the same GetDeploying median than the cost stack's, $3.98 per GPU-hour (both readings and their dates are in this course's claims table), is 8 × 3.98 × 24 = $764.16, or **$0.40/M**: the same tokens at 2.0× the cost, because the round $2 assumed above is a course stand-in, not a market rent.

Against R1's $2.19/M list price, that gap is the whole story of the 2025 margin. No source gives a fixed fall in API prices that this arithmetic explains, so read no "x% a year" into it. What is sourced is narrower. Epoch AI's data insight [LLM inference prices have fallen rapidly but unequally across tasks](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends) measures the price of *reaching a fixed benchmark score*: it fell fast, at rates that vary widely with the milestone, and fastest in the most recent year, which Epoch says may not persist. That is the price of a capability, not any one model's list price: DeepSeek's own flagship, deepseek-v4-pro, lists $3.96 per million output tokens at peak hours and $1.98 off-peak, against R1's $2.19.

As a formula: **$/Mtok = ($/GPU-hour × GPUs) ÷ (tok/s × 3,600) × 10⁶**, with tok/s counted within the SLO you set. A hypothetical 8-GPU node at $2 per GPU-hour that delivers 20,000 tok/s costs 16 ÷ 72,000,000 × 10⁶ = **$0.22/Mtok**. Run it backwards for a price target: the node needs ($/hour ÷ target $/Mtok) × 10⁶ ÷ 3,600 tok/s, so $0.10/Mtok asks for 16 ÷ 0.10 × 10⁶ ÷ 3,600 ≈ **44,400 tok/s**. Tokens per megawatt divides the same throughput by power instead of price: a hypothetical rack drawing 0.12 MW that delivers 600,000 tok/s makes **5,000,000 tok/s per MW**.

## The levers, priced

Every T5/T6 technique restated as a unit-economics lever: **caching** (DeepSeek's 56.3% hit: over half the input tokens billed at $0.14/M instead of $0.55/M, and their prefill never run), **batching** (amortize weights), **quantization** (fewer bytes per token → more tokens per GPU-hour; NVFP4 is ~1.8× fewer bytes than FP8, not 2×, [T6.L5](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t6.l5)), **wide EP** (5× output throughput, [T6.L2](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t6.l2)), **disaggregation** (right-sized phase fleets, [T6.L3](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t6.l3)), **MTP** (2–3× interactivity at the same fleet, [T6.L6](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t6.l6)). Hardware generations enter through tok/s/$ and tok/MW — vendor multipliers like the 35× above are claims at one operating point, and [T7.L3](https://kernelspace.naigap.com/lesson/t7.l3)'s regional caveat applies.

- **$0.20/M** — output tokens, SGLang 96×H100 (One decode node at $2/GPU-hr, TTFT 2–5 s and ITL about 100 ms, vs $2.19/M list price.)
- **$3.96/M** — deepseek-v4-pro output tokens, peak hours (Against R1's $2.19/M. The price of a capability falls fast (Epoch AI); a flagship's list price need not.)

**Q1. DeepSeek's 545% margin day rested primarily on…**

- (o1) Serving a small model, with each token needing few operations and margin coming from model size
- (o2) An accelerator cost basis far below the market rate, with almost any traffic volume producing a large margin
- (o3) Free or subsidized electricity, with power being the dominant cost line in a serving business
- (o4) Systems levers such as wide expert-parallel goodput, a high cache-hit share and output pricing

**Q2. Why are output tokens priced 3–5× input tokens?**

- (o1) Providers recover prefill compute through output pricing, and the price includes processing the whole prompt
- (o2) Decode is serial and bandwidth-bound while input is parallel and cached, and outputs carry the marginal cost
- (o3) Outputs are shorter than prompts, and per-request overhead spread over fewer tokens makes each one costlier
- (o4) Users value generated text more than prompts, and providers price on perceived value rather than cost

**Q3. tok/MW matters because…**

- (o1) Operators publish energy efficiency to meet sustainability goals, and power does not limit capacity
- (o2) A site's power envelope is fixed, and tokens per megawatt turns each generation into sellable output
- (o3) It tracks tokens per dollar exactly, and the two metrics rank each hardware generation the same way
- (o4) Electricity is the largest line in the cost stack, and it dominates the bill ahead of accelerator rental

**Q4. At a fixed hourly rate, an engine's cost per token is driven most directly by…**

- (o1) The chip's hourly rate, with a rented or amortized card costing the same per hour regardless of what it runs
- (o2) The number of chips in the fleet, with each added chip raising the hourly bill and the cost of each token
- (o3) The peak utilization reached in the busiest minute, which shows how efficiently the silicon can be used
- (o4) Goodput per billed chip hour, meaning tokens delivered within the latency target per hour
