# kernelspace: audit report

> This is the audit report of the course, finished on 2026-11-18, about the course at master d478f91 (exported 2026-10-30) and the live site as it was walked then. It is a plan, not a record: of its waves only Wave 0 has been built ([docs/specs/k0.md](../specs/k0.md)); waves 1 to 5 are planned in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md), and their scope and order are the owner's open decision. The text below is unchanged apart from this note and the plain descriptions that replace references to files not in the repository. Line numbers that roadmap.md cites in this report count from the original, which had no note: in this copy each is two lines lower. What has changed since is in [after-wave-0.md](after-wave-0.md).

Audit date 2026-11-18. Audited: the export of `master` at d478f91 (2026-10-30) and the live site https://kernelspace.naigap.com. Written from the notes of twenty-three examiners (nine track readers, a frame reader, a labs reader, a sequence examiner, six currency examiners, two landscape examiners, two walkers of the live site and a teaching reviewer), then tested by three checkers who tried to refute the draft: one against the course's own files (about 520 claims), one against primary sources fetched on 2026-11-18 (87 claims), one arguing the owner's side against the recommendations (127 items). They returned 70 corrections. All are applied here, three of them only in part (section 8).

Each finding has an id and is stated once: F and A (facts; A is the appendix), O (order), L (where a learner gets lost), D (the teaching), N (a new chapter), M (a development in the field). Lesson ids are lower case (t5.l7); tracks are upper case (T5). Words in double quotes after a lesson id are the lesson's own. "Read 2026-11-18" means an examiner or the web checker fetched the source that day; I fetched nothing myself.

Two cautions before acting on any row. The export is 19 days older than the audit, nobody diffed `master` since, and the walkers did not record which build the live site served. And the correction tables are a worklist, not text to paste: check each row at its source as you apply it (section 8 says why).

## 1. The verdict

1. As one free, self-paced path from a cache line to a priced fleet for a Java or Python engineer, kernelspace has no rival among the 62 courses, books and guides the landscape examiner opened; that is its claim to "best", and today it holds for the whole and not for the parts.
2. Part by part it is first where nobody has written the course yet (engine mechanics in t5.l4 to t5.l7 with labs 02, 06 and 07; T6; the exercises of T7), behind the standard texts where the field is old (R, T0 to T3) and behind the GPU courses on practice (T4), so a learner with no systems background meets the weakest tracks first.
3. It owns teaching devices that none of those 62 has together; two are everywhere (a reason for every wrong option, lab checks calibrated against wrong designs) and the rest are rare: 39 of 69 lessons have no question before the telling, 9 have a find-the-false-sentence block, 9 have a simulator task graded against a prediction.
4. Its prose is not up to date: of 695 checks made on 2026-11-18, about 78 found a statement that had gone out of date and about 43 one that was wrong when written (about 88 more were imprecise and about 17 could not be checked), because the registry of 366 dated claims is sound at its pins and lesson prose and chips do not read from it.
5. The reference engine moved 17 days after the audited commit: vLLM v0.34.0 (2026-11-16) made the default KV block 64 tokens on CUDA and sizes the pool by reserve-then-commit, and the course says 16 in at least nine places (F4).
6. Several things a learner is graded on are wrong: a graded option in t0.l6 teaches that a CPU flame graph shows waiting (F1), the toy executor does not compile (F2), t5.l5's first figure shows copy-on-write on a block that is never written to (F3), and t7.l2 grades "the knee" by two different definitions on one page (L7).
7. The way in is broken on the live site: placement scores a skipped Rust question as a miss and then prints "Start at T5" above a button that says "Start at R.L1", an arrow key pressed inside a simulator leaves the lesson and loses the work, and the first button after Boot serves untaught items with the answers printed (L1 to L3).
8. The best hands-on pieces are built and off the road: the lesson called "EXAM: Read the PagedAttention Paper" has no link to the paper and can be passed without opening it, real engine source is opened with the learner only after labs 06 and 07 are finished, and the real-model run and the capstone's tensor engine are a step of no path (D2, D9).
9. Three things later lessons lean on are taught nowhere (what happens to one request, how a token is chosen and when generation stops, open against closed loop); section 5 answers with five short lessons and about a dozen sections, about two hours now and three in all, where the draft of this report asked for about twenty lessons.
10. Most of the work is the standard of the course's own best lessons (t5.l7, t6.l12, r.l9, t1.l4) applied to the others and the registry connected to the prose; before the months of it, watch five newcomers on the first three lessons, because no learner but the owner has used the course yet.

## 2. The owner's five questions

### 2.1 Is the content up to date?

**No in the prose; yes in the registry. From T4 upward the engines, hardware and prices moved under the lessons. Below T4 fewer facts are dated, but those about Python, Java and Rust tooling have moved too; R's compiler claims all held.**

| Area checked on 2026-11-18 | Checked | Out of date | Wrong when written | Imprecise | Could not be checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPU and memory hardware | 103 | 8 | 4 | 17 | 3 |
| Inference engines | 63 | 12 | 10 | 9 | 6 |
| Models, architectures, quantization | 167 | 11 | 5 | 12 | 0 |
| Serving at very large scale | 216 | 13 | 10 | 15 | 4 |
| Prices, benchmarks, economics | 60 | 14 | 2 | 13 | 2 |
| Rust, operating systems, tooling | 86 | 20 | 12 | 22 | 2 |

Read the table as counts, not rates: the lesson statements were chosen because a reader had flagged them, and some were checked by more than one examiner. "Imprecise" means true only with a qualifier the lesson omits. The split is the examiners' own tally where they made one (engines, hardware, serving) and a recount of their status cells elsewhere; it sums to about 226 where the draft's single column "not current" said 233, so every figure is approximate. Eighteen rows were marked must-teach; they reduce to nine developments (M1 to M9, section 5.5).

- **The registry is good and does not protect the lessons.** Every sourced claim the examiners re-read still matches its source at its pin. About ten are out of date at head: six rental prices, the vLLM block size, glibc's bins, two claim boundaries (F4, A24, A25). None is past its time-to-live; the first 11 expire on 2026-12-04 and 17 more on 2026-12-13. But a lesson's numbers are plain strings (`StatChipData` has no claim id; three numeric prequestions do carry one, and t1.l4's is keyed to the block-size claim), 12 of 69 lessons have a knowledge component that cites any claim, and the "last verified" badge is a typed month: seven of the eleven lessons badged 2026-08 were edited by errata in October.
- **The calendar is the wrong alarm for engines.** vLLM shipped 23 feature releases in twelve months. A pinned default became false on the main branch 31 days after it was verified and in a release 42 days after; its 60-day life would have put it in a deploy log on day 60. A tool the course recommends has been archived for eleven months and no claim covered it (F5).
- **Most of what is wrong was never a matter of date.** By the errata's own `kind` field, 72 of the 77 published errata record that the course was wrong when written and 5 that the field moved; 55 are of kinds no freshness check could have caught (arithmetic, mechanisms, misquoted figures, units, analogies). This audit found the same kinds again after the October sweep: errors of fact in every T5 lesson from L2 to L10, three checkable errors and a mislabel in t1.l3's listing (F6), T3 listings that fail when run (F2, A33, A34), a paper's numbers set against the wrong baselines (A1).
- **Below T4 the changes are yearly but real** (F13, F14, A25, A37). The conditional-return rejection that r.l4 and r.l9 teach is true on stable Rust 1.100.0 and already false on nightly (A47). R has no errata and no claims; its reader compiled every snippet on rustc 1.96.0 and found every compiler claim right.
- **The thesis aged better than the facts.** In twelve months the engines turned the course's analogies into mechanisms (vLLM and SGLang release notes, read 2026-11-18): reserve-then-commit for the KV pool (vLLM v0.34.0, default on CUDA and ROCm), paging of KV to host memory for sparse-attention models (HiSparse, v0.30.0), opt-in paging of expert weights on a single GPU (SGLang v0.5.23), an experimental checkpoint and restore of a warm single-GPU engine (v0.31.0). KV offload is named in t6.l3 and t5.l6 and worked in neither; the other three are in no lesson, and no other course is placed to teach them as what they are.

### 2.2 Does the course make sense?

**Yes in its spine. No in its routing, and its best built pieces are on no route.**

- The spine (machine, memory in C, the OS, the GPU, serving, scale, money) is right, and the road t1.l3, t1.l4, t2.l2, t2.l3, t2.l7, t5.l5 is the best-prepared idea in the course. OSTEP and CS:APP stop at the OS; the serving courses start at the paper. A 2026 Tübingen seminar lists T5 and T6's topics almost item for item, and ASPLOS '26's session titles read like their table of contents (both read 2026-11-18).
- What the serving half draws from the lower half is narrow and well chosen: six lessons by a reading of the prose (t1.l3, t1.l4, t2.l2 to t2.l4, t4.l3), and the course's own prerequisite graph adds t4.l7, t4.l2 and t2.l1. Of the rest: t1.l5 is used only inside T3; t0.l6 by one aside in t6.l1; t2.l6 is what t6.l2 rests dual-batch overlap on; t3.l5 is the one T3 lesson that T6 requires (t6.l10). t4.l1, t4.l4, t4.l5 and t4.l6 (110 declared minutes) are depth, not prerequisite. No lesson or track is marked optional or "not needed for serving"; the word is used only for deep dives, field labs, lab 02's adapter stage and Boot's forms.
- The routing does not make sense: four lesson orders with two on screen at once, a finale on no path, two things called a capstone, four totals for the hours, two offered paths that can earn no ring (L8).
- The pieces that would answer "is this real?" are built and off the default road. Capstone Zero (a seven-step engine on a toy two-layer transformer) and the Fleet's real-engine panel (the learner's lab 06 scheduler over Qwen3-0.6B with wall-clock TTFT) are steps of no path; the production twins are shown only after labs 06 and 07; and one deployment is sized, batched and priced end to end only in the bring-your-own-service capstone, which needs the learner's own trace (D2, D9, N9).
- The loop is built and unvalidated: no design-partner report, no screen-reader pass, a leaderboard with 0 entries, two field labs labelled "Not run on a GPU yet".

### 2.3 Is it easy to follow?

**Not at the start, not in T6 and T7, and not at several points of the live site. Where the course's own devices are in use (t1.l4, t5.l4 to t5.l7, r.l3, r.l4, r.l8, r.l9) it is easier to follow than its rivals.**

- A Python engineer with one hour finished Boot, t0.l1 and half of t0.l2. Continuous batching, the thing in the site's title, is the tenth lesson of 29 on the path the site gave them, four to five hours in. No screen said so.
- The first lesson carries 24 new concepts and relies on 40 the course has not taught (L4); the second on the default path, r.l1, is a 356-word card (D1).
- The median lesson puts roughly 650 to 750 words (two counts) before the first thing to do after its opening; the five best start in under 300 (t5.l7, r.l8, r.l9, t1.l4, t5.l2).
- T6 and T7 have no question before the telling, three diagrams in sixteen lessons, and the point in a collapsed block after the quiz (D4). There is nowhere to look a word up (L5).
- The walkers named where they would have closed the tab: L1, L3, L6, L9, and Boot's last form (L15). What they valued came from doing: the practitioner counted six things learned in about three hours and "Five of the six came from an interaction, not from the prose".

### 2.4 Does it explain all concepts properly and in sequence?

**No. Inside a track the order is mostly sound. Across the course the serving vocabulary is used about twenty lessons before it is defined and there is nowhere to look it up, and a short list of ideas that T4 to T7 stand on is explained in no lesson.**

- Of 61 tracked serving terms, 14 are never defined in a lesson (some fairly: batch, sequence, percentile) and 40 are used before their definition, by a median of about 20 lessons; 21 are first used in t0.l1. The sequence examiner's caveat belongs with the numbers: a late definition is not always a fault, and a preview was counted as a use. The fault is that the learner has nowhere to look in the meantime (L5).
- Explained in no lesson: what a FLOP is; tensor cores; bandwidth as distinct from latency; what a query, a key, a value and a head are for, and the causal mask (O16); softmax as a distribution, temperature and top-p (greedy argmax and stopping at EOS are taught only in Capstone Zero); the kernel boundary; what RDMA and a collective are (their bandwidth ladder is taught); utilisation against waiting time, and Little's law as L = λW (t7.l2 writes its capacity form once, in exercise feedback); grammars and pushdown automata; and, for T3 and lab 05 only, traits and generics.
- Out of order: O1 to O16. The largest is that the serving vocabulary is used from the first lesson and defined at lessons 44 to 46 of 69.
- "Properly" fails in three ways that are not about order: mechanisms asserted where the best lesson derives them (D4); simulators that are typed-in formulas presented as measurements (D5); labs that cannot detect the property they say they teach (D9).
- Nothing locks and every ticket can be passed from its own lesson, so no learner is stopped. The sequence examiner found 20 places where they must look elsewhere to understand what they are reading, and the only elsewhere is the open web.

### 2.5 Are there new chapters worth adding?

**Yes, but few. Two short lessons and one paragraph are owed to later lessons (N1, N3, N4); three more lessons earn their place (N9, N16, and N2 at the head of T3); the other gaps are sections inside existing lessons, two proposals wait on the owner, and the lab work is mostly routing what is already built.** Section 5, with a budget.
